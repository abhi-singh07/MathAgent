**Problem Statement:**
A 4-inch by 6-inch picture is enlarged by tripling its dimensions. A 2-inch-wide border is added around each side. We need to find the minimum number of linear feet of framing required to go around the perimeter of
the border. Framing is sold in one-foot increments.

**Step-by-Step Solution:**

1. **Calculate the Enlarged Picture Dimensions:**
   - Original dimensions: 4 inches (width) × 6 inches (height)
   - Enlargement factor: 3
   - New dimensions after enlargement:
     - Width: \(4 \times 3 = 12\)
inches
     - Height: \(6 \times 3 = 18\) inches

2. **Add the Border:**
   - Border width: 2 inches on each side
   - Total addition to each dimension:
     - Width: \(2 \times 2 = 4\) inches
     - Height: \(2 \times 2 = 4\) inches
   - Final dimensions including the border:
     - Width: \(12 + 4
= 16\) inches
     - Height: \(18 + 4 = 22\) inches

3. **Calculate the Perimeter:**
   - Perimeter formula: \(2 \times (\text{Width} + \text{Height})\)
   - Perimeter: \(2 \times (16 + 22) = 2 \times 38 = 76\) inches

4. **Convert Perimeter to Feet:**
   - Since \(1 \text{ foot} = 12 \text{
inches}\):
   - Perimeter in feet: \( \frac{76}{12} = 6.\overline{3} \) feet

5. **Determine Framing Needed:**
   - Framing is sold in one-foot increments
   - We need to round up to the next whole number
   - Minimum framing required: \( \lceil 6.\overline{3} \rceil = 7 \) feet

**Final
Answer:**
\boxed{7}
