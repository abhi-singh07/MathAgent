**Problem Statement:**
Quadrilateral \(ABCD\) is a trapezoid
with \(AB\) parallel to \(CD\). We know \(AB = 20\) and \(CD = 12\). What is the ratio of the area of triangle \(ACB\) to the area of the trapezoid \(ABCD\)? Express your answer as a common fraction.

**Step-by-Step Solution:**

1. **Understand the Trapezoid and Triangle Areas:**
   - A trapezoid
has two parallel sides (\(AB\) and \(CD\)) and the other two sides (\(AD\) and \(BC\)) that may or may not be parallel.
   - The area of a trapezoid is given by the formula:
     \[
     \text{Area}_{\text{trapezoid}} = \frac{(AB + CD)}{2} \times h
     \]
     where \(h\) is the height (the
perpendicular distance between the two bases \(AB\) and \(CD\)).

2. **Calculate the Area of the Trapezoid:**
   - Given \(AB = 20\) and \(CD = 12\), plug these into the formula:
     \[
     \text{Area}_{\text{trapezoid}} = \frac{(20 + 12)}{2} \times h = 16h
     \]

3. **Determine the Area of
Triangle \(ACB\):**
   - Triangle \(ACB\) shares the base \(AB\) with the trapezoid and has the same height \(h\).
   - The area of triangle \(ACB\) is:
     \[
     \text{Area}_{\triangle ACB} = \frac{1}{2} \times AB \times h = \frac{1}{2} \times 20 \times h = 10h
     \]

4. **Find the Ratio of
the Areas:**
   - The ratio of the area of triangle \(ACB\) to the area of the trapezoid \(ABCD\) is:
     \[
     \text{Ratio} = \frac{\text{Area}_{\triangle ACB}}{\text{Area}_{\text{trapezoid}}} = \frac{10h}{16h} = \frac{10}{16} = \frac{5}{8}
     \]
   - Notice that the height \(h\) cancels out,
so the ratio is independent of the height.

**Final Answer:**
\[
\boxed{\dfrac{5}{8}}
\]
