Problem: Both roots of the quadratic equation $x^2 - 63 x + k = 0$ are prime numbers. How many possible values of $k$ are there?
 
----------------------------------------
assistant: Solution Plan:

1. Key Idea:
   - We use Vieta's formulas: if the roots of x² − 63x + k = 0 are p and q, then:
     • p + q = 63
     • p × q = k
   - We must find primes p and q that satisfy p + q = 63. The value(s) of k will then be p × q.

2. Step-by-Step Reasoning:
   1) By definition, prime
numbers (aside from 2) are odd, so the sum of two odd primes (if both are greater than 2) would be even. But 63 is odd. Hence, one of the primes must be 2.  
   2) If p = 2, then q = 63 – 2 = 61. Both 2 and 61 are prime.  
   3) Therefore, the only possible pair of prime roots is (2, 61) (or (61,
2)).  
   4) The corresponding value of k is 2 × 61 = 122.  
   5) Hence, there is exactly 1 possible value of k.

3. Justification of Values:
   - We used the fact that 2 is the only even prime. This justifies checking pairs where one root is 2 and the other is 63 – 2.  

4. Alternative Approaches
or Uncertainties:
   - We could have also enumerated all primes under 63 and checked which pairs sum to 63. This would yield the same result.  
   - There is no ambiguity in prime definitions or sums for numbers up to 63, so there are no additional uncertainties to address.
