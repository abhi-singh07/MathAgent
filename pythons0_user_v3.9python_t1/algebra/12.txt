**Problem:**  
In 1960, there were 450,000 cases of measles reported in the U.S. In 1996, there were 500 cases reported.
How many cases of measles would have been reported in 1987 if the number of cases reported from 1960 to 1996 decreased linearly?

---

**Key Idea:**  
We can model the decrease in measles cases as a linear function. The linear decrease can be represented by the equation of a straight
line.

---

**Step 1: Determine the Total Time Span**  
From 1960 to 1996 is:  
\[ 1996 - 1960 = 36 \text{ years} \]

---

**Step 2: Calculate the Total Decrease in Cases**  
The decrease from 450,000 to 500 cases is:  
\[ 450,000 - 500 = 449,500 \text{ cases} \]

---

**Step 3: Find the Annual Rate
of Decrease**  
Assuming a linear decrease, the annual decrease rate \( r \) is:  
\[ r = \frac{449,500}{36} \approx 12,486.11 \text{ cases per year} \]

---

**Step 4: Determine the Number of Years from 1960 to 1987**  
\[ 1987 - 1960 = 27 \text{ years} \]

---

**Step 5: Calculate the Decrease by
1987**  
\[ \text{Total Decrease} = 27 \times 12,486.11 = 337,125 \text{ cases} \]

---

**Step 6: Find the Number of Cases in 1987**  
\[ \text{Cases in 1987} = 450,000 - 337,125 = 112,875 \]

---

**Final Answer:**  
The number of measles cases reported in 1987 would have been \(\boxed{112875}\)
cases.
