To determine the value of \( a \) such that \( ax^2 + 15x + 4 \) is the square of a binomial, we compare it to the expansion of \( (bx + c)^2 \).

1. **Set up the comparison:**
   \[
   ax^2 + 15x + 4 = (bx + c)^2 = b^2x^2 + 2bcx + c^2
  
\]

2. **Equate coefficients:**
   - For \( x^2 \) terms: \( a = b^2 \)
   - For \( x \) terms: \( 15 = 2bc \)
   - For constant terms: \( 4 = c^2 \)

3. **Solve for \( c \):**
   \[
   c^2 = 4 \implies c = \pm 2
   \]

4. **Find \( b \) using \( c \):**
   - If \( c = 2 \):
     \[
     15 = 2b(2)
\implies 15 = 4b \implies b = \frac{15}{4}
     \]
   - If \( c = -2 \):
     \[
     15 = 2b(-2) \implies 15 = -4b \implies b = -\frac{15}{4}
     \]

5. **Determine \( a \) using \( b \):**
   \[
   a = b^2 = \left(\frac{15}{4}\right)^2 = \frac{225}{16}
   \]
   (The same result occurs if \( b =
-\frac{15}{4} \) since squaring removes the negative sign.)

Thus, the value of \( a \) that makes \( ax^2 + 15x + 4 \) a perfect square is \( \frac{225}{16} \).

Solution: The square of the binomial $rx+s$ is  \[(rx+s)^2=r^2x^2+2rsx+s^2.\]If this is equal to $ax^2+15x+4$, then $s$ must be either 2 or -2.  Since $(rx+s)^2=(-rx-s)^2$, we may choose either $s=2$ or $s=-2$, and the solution will be the same.  We choose $s=2$.

The square of $rx+2$ is  \[(rx+2)^2=r^2x^2+4rx+4.\]If this is equal to $ax^2+15x+4$ then we must have $15=4r$ or $r=\frac{15}4$.  This gives our square: \[\left(\frac{15}4x+2\right)^2=\frac{225}{16}x^2+15x+4.\]Therefore $a=\boxed{\frac{225}{16}}$.
