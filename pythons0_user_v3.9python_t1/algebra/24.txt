Given that \( f(x) = x^k \) where \( k < 0 \), what is the range of \( f(x) \) on the interval \([1, \infty)\)?

2. **Evaluate at the Lower Bound of the Interval:**
   - The interval given is \([1, \infty)\).
   - Evaluate \( f(x) \) at \( x = 1 \):
     \[
   
 f(1) = 1^k = 1
     \]
   - So, at \( x = 1 \), \( f(x) = 1 \).

   - As \( x \) increases beyond 1, \( x^{|k|} \) increases because \( |k| > 0 \).
   - Consequently, \( \frac{1}{x^{|k|}} \) decreases as \( x \) increases.

4. **Determine the Limit as
\( x \) Approaches Infinity:**
   - To understand the behavior of \( f(x) \) as \( x \) becomes very large:
     \[
     \lim_{x \to \infty} f(x) = \lim_{x \to \infty} \frac{1}{x^{|k|}} = 0
     \]
   - This means that as \( x \) approaches infinity, \( f(x) \) approaches 0.

5. **Determine the
Range:**
   - At \( x = 1 \), \( f(x) = 1 \).
   - As \( x \) increases, \( f(x) \) decreases from 1 towards 0 but never actually reaches 0.
