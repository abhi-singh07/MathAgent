1:** Understand the properties of an arithmetic sequence.

\[
\text{Second term} - \text{First term} = \text{Third term} - \text{Second term}
\]

**Step 2:** Set up the equation using the given
terms.

Given:
- First term: \( \frac{1}{2} \)
- Second term: \( x - 1 \)
- Third term: \( 3x \)

According to the arithmetic sequence property:
\[
(x - 1) - \frac{1}{2} = 3x - (x - 1)
\]

**Step 3:** Simplify both sides of the equation.

Left side:
\[
(x - 1) - \frac{1}{2} = x - 1 - \frac{1}{2} = x
- \frac{3}{2}
\]

Right side:
\[
3x - (x - 1) = 3x - x + 1 = 2x + 1
\]

So, the equation becomes:
\[
x - \frac{3}{2} = 2x + 1
\]

**Step 4:** Solve for \( x \).

Subtract \( x \) from both sides:
\[
-\frac{3}{2} = x + 1
\]

Subtract 1 from both sides:
\[
x = -\frac{3}{2} - 1 =
-\frac{5}{2}
\]

**Step 5:** Verify the solution.

Plug \( x = -\frac{5}{2} \) back into the terms:
- Second term: \( x - 1 = -\frac{5}{2} - 1 = -\frac{7}{2} \)
- Third term: \( 3x = 3 \times -\frac{5}{2} = -\frac{15}{2} \)

Check the common difference:
\[
-\frac{7}{2} - \frac{1}{2} = -\frac{8}{2} =
-4
\]
\[
-\frac{15}{2} - \left(-\frac{7}{2}\right) = -\frac{15}{2} + \frac{7}{2} = -\frac{8}{2} = -4
