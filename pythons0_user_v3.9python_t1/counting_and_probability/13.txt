To solve the problem, we start by defining the number of red socks as \( r \) and blue socks as \( b \), with the total number
of socks \( t = r + b \leq 1991 \). The given probability condition translates to:

\[
\frac{\binom{r}{2} + \binom{b}{2}}{\binom{t}{2}} = \frac{1}{2}
\]

This simplifies to:

\[
\frac{r(r-1) + b(b-1)}{t(t-1)} = \frac{1}{2}
\]

Substituting \( b = t - r \) and simplifying, we find that \( t \) must
be a perfect square. The largest perfect square less than or equal to 1991 is \( 44^2 = 1936 \). This leads to:

\[
r = \frac{44^2 + 44}{2} = 990
\]

Thus, the largest possible number of red socks is:

\[
\boxed{990}
\]
