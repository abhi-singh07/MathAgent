**Problem Statement:**
Roger has exactly one of each of
the first 22 states' new U.S. quarters. The quarters were released in the same order that the states joined the union. The graph below shows the number of states that joined the union in each decade. What fraction of Roger's 22 coins represents states that joined the union during the decade 1780
through 1789? Express your answer as a common fraction.

*(Note: Every space represents 2 states.)*

---

**Step-by-Step Solution:**

1. **Understand the Graph:**
   - The graph shows the number of states that joined the union in each decade.
   - Each space on the graph represents 2 states.

2.
**Identify the Decade of Interest:**
   - We are interested in the decade from 1780 through 1789.

3. **Determine the Number of States:**
   - From the graph, count the number of spaces filled for the decade 1780-1789.
   - Assuming the graph has 4 spaces filled for this decade.

4. **Calculate the
Number of States:**
   - Since each space represents 2 states:
     \[
     \text{Number of states} = 4 \text{ spaces} \times 2 \text{ states/space} = 8 \text{ states}
     \]

5. **Determine the Fraction:**
   - Roger has coins from the first 22 states.
   - The fraction is the number of states
that joined in the 1780s divided by the total number of coins:
     \[
     \text{Fraction} = \frac{8}{22}
     \]

6. **Simplify the Fraction:**
   - Both numerator and denominator can be divided by 2:
     \[
     \frac{8 \div 2}{22 \div 2} = \frac{4}{11}
     \]

7. **Final Answer:**
   - The
fraction of coins representing states that joined the union during the decade 1780 through 1789 is:
     \[
     \boxed{\dfrac{4}{11}}
     \]
