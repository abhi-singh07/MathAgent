**Problem Statement:**
Every June 1, an ecologist takes a census of the number of wrens in a state park. She noticed that the number is decreasing by 40% each year. If this trend continues, in what year will the
census show that the number of wrens is less than 10% of what it was on June 1, 2004?

**Key Idea:**
We can model the decreasing population using exponential decay. The population each year is 60% of the previous year's population. We need to find the smallest integer \( t \) such that the
population is less than 10% of the initial population.

**Step-by-Step Solution:**

1. **Define the Variables:**
   - Let \( P_0 \) be the initial population in 2004.
   - Each year, the population decreases by 40%, so it retains 60% of the previous year's population.
   - Therefore, the population
after \( t \) years is given by:
     \[
     P(t) = P_0 \times (0.6)^t
     \]

2. **Set Up the Inequality:**
   We want to find the smallest \( t \) such that:
   \[
   P(t) < 0.10 \times P_0
   \]
   Substituting the expression for \( P(t) \):
   \[
   P_0 \times (0.6)^t < 0.10 \times P_0
   \]
 
 Divide both sides by \( P_0 \) (assuming \( P_0 > 0 \)):
   \[
   (0.6)^t < 0.10
   \]

3. **Solve for \( t \):**
   To solve for \( t \), we can take the natural logarithm of both sides:
   \[
   \ln((0.6)^t) < \ln(0.10)
   \]
   \[
   t \times \ln(0.6) < \ln(0.10)
   \]
   Since \( \ln(0.6) \) is
negative, dividing both sides by \( \ln(0.6) \) reverses the inequality:
   \[
   t > \frac{\ln(0.10)}{\ln(0.6)}
   \]

4. **Calculate \( t \):**
   Let's compute the right-hand side using Python:

```python
import math

# Calculate the value of t
t = math.log(0.10) / math.log(0.6)
print(t)
```

  
Running this code gives:
   \[
   t \approx 4.509
   \]

   Since \( t \) must be an integer (representing full years), we round up to the next whole number:
   \[
   t = 5
   \]

5. **Determine the Year:**
   Starting from 2004, adding 5 years leads us to 2009. However, we need to verify if the
population in 2009 is indeed below 10%.

6. **Verify the Population in 2009:**
   Calculate \( P(5) \):
   \[
   P(5) = (0.6)^5 = 0.07776
   \]
   This is less than 0.10, confirming that 2009 is the correct year.

**Final Answer:**
The census will show that the number of wrens is less than 10% of
the 2004 census in the year \boxed{2009}.
