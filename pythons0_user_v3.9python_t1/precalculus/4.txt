   - Use the identity for \(\tan(2\theta)\) to find \(\tan(\theta)\):
     \[
     \tan(2\theta) = \frac{2\tan(\theta)}{1 - \tan^2(\theta)} = -\frac{24}{7}
     \]
   -
Let \(t = \tan(\theta)\), solve the quadratic equation:
     \[
     \frac{2t}{1 - t^2} = -\frac{24}{7}
     \]
     Solving this gives \(t = \frac{4}{3}\) (since \(\theta\) is in the first quadrant, \(\tan(\theta)\) is positive).

4. **Verify Using Eigenvectors**:
   - The eigenvector corresponding
to eigenvalue 1 gives the direction of the reflection line.
   - Solving \((A - I)\mathbf{v} = 0\) confirms the ratio \(\frac{y}{x} = \frac{4}{3}\).

### Final Answer:
The ratio \(\frac{y}{x}\) is \boxed{\dfrac{4}{3}}.
----------------------------------------
Solution: Note that the reflecting $\begin{pmatrix} x \\ y \end{pmatrix}$ over itself results in itself, so
\[\begin{pmatrix} -\frac{7}{25} & \frac{24}{25} \\ \frac{24}{25} & \frac{7}{25} \end{pmatrix} \begin{pmatrix} x \\ y \end{pmatrix} = \begin{pmatrix} x \\ y \end{pmatrix}.\]Then $-\frac{7}{25} x + \frac{24}{25} y = x$ and $\frac{24}{25} x + \frac{7}{25} y = y.$  Both equations lead to $\frac{y}{x} = \boxed{\frac{4}{3}}.$
